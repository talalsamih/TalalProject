Drawing on linguistic evidence embedded in Arabic—particularly the etymological connection between Allah (الله) and Alat (آلة, meaning "machine")—the book argues that reality is fundamentally computational in nature, consciousness is a form of computation, and God is the universal processor from which all existence emerges.

Through an interdisciplinary approach combining linguistics, philosophy, theology, quantum physics, information theory, and consciousness studies, the book demonstrates how ancient wisdom preserved in sacred language converges with cutting-edge scientific understanding. Key insights include: the parallel between worship (Tasbih) and computation (Tahsib), the body as hardware constraining consciousness-as-software, death as energy transformation and data return, and the Day of Judgment as the ultimate computational reckoning.

The theory addresses fundamental questions in philosophy of mind, particularly the hard problem of consciousness, by proposing that subjective experience is the intrinsic quality of information processing. It reconciles theological and scientific worldviews by revealing them as different languages describing the same computational reality. With implications spanning artificial intelligence, physics, neuroscience, and spirituality, this work offers a paradigm-shifting perspective on existence itself—one that transforms how we understand our place in a fundamentally computational universe.
